std.cmp

std.cmp — comparison and ordering: the Ordering enum, the Eq and Ord protocols, and the min/max/clamp family. Stability tier: core.

Decided here and recorded (sc01): f64 ordering. Its Ord is total_cmp (IEEE totalOrder), so sorting floats is legal and NaN has one home (above +inf). See total_cmp for the two documented approximations at the current pins.

f64 HAS AN Eq NOW, AND sc44 REVERSED sc01 TO GIVE IT ONE. This header said for forty-three sprints that f64 has no Eq because "IEEE partial comparison stays on the builtin operators". [type.trait.op] (s155, wolf-lang#5) is what retires that sentence: since the operator bridge landed, == on a type parameter IS Eq.eq, so "stays on the builtin operator" and "goes through Eq" stopped being two different places. Withholding the impl would not have kept a partial order out of a total protocol; it would only have made f64 the one primitive whose == does not work under a bound, while < under the same bound did. The impl's body is self == other — the clause's rule that a primitive impl IS the builtin operation — and the law caveat is stated on the impl itself: reflexivity fails at NaN, which is exactly what the builtin does and is why Ord for f64 is total_cmp and not this.

INTERIM SPELLINGS (each filed, flipped on the pin bump that fixes it):

  • The absence-spelling interim is RETIRED (F-0003, dead at the sc05 pins, renamed tree-wide at sc06): a lowercase bare tag resolves in every implementation. Until sc12 this line still described the interim, in a half-renamed sentence that said the source said the opposite of what it says.
  • Ord requires Eq by doctrine, and the requirement is still DOCUMENTARY, but for a smaller reason than it was. s155 gave the grammar a trait_item with an = form (the alias bound, [type.trait.op.alias]) and no supertrait clause, so composition this edition is an alias listing both and not trait Ord: Eq. F-0004's operator half is RULED — ==, != and the relationals dispatch through Eq/Ord by name on a type parameter or a user type, which is what std.ops is written against — and its supertrait half is what remains open.
  • F-0006 IS RETIRED AND impl Ord for str SHIPS. The finding said "wolfc rejects < on str (E0409) and one rejected body poisons every test that imports this module". Re-measured at the sc44 pins on all three lanes: cmp.Ord.cmp("a", "b").is_lt() is true on the checked tier, the body passes the static ladder on every lane, and nothing is poisoned. The impl is written here in the same shape as Ord for int — two relational tests and a fallthrough — and the order it reports is the language's byte-lexicographic one ([mem.str.order]), which std.sort's sort_strs already documents is not alphabetical in any human language (F-0019 owns collation).

total_cmpfnpub

fn total_cmp(a: f64, b: f64) -> self.Ordering · regions (-, -) -> ρ_caller

IEEE totalOrder over floats, monomorphic: -inf < negative reals < -0.0 < +0.0 < positive reals < +inf < NaN. Total: no error row, no traps (f64 division by zero is IEEE, not a trap — X3 governs integers).

Two documented approximations at the current pins (no f64 bit intrinsics exist to do better): NaN is one value — the sign and payload of a NaN are not observed, so every NaN sorts above +inf and all NaNs compare Equal; totalOrder's -NaN-below--inf home is not represented.

Each line is true: total_cmp(1.0, 2.0) agrees with total_cmp(-1.0e9, 3.5) (both Less), total_cmp(2.0, 1.0) with total_cmp(0.0 / 0.0, 1.0) (both Greater — NaN above every real), total_cmp(4.5, 4.5) with total_cmp(0.0 / 0.0, 0.0 / 0.0) (both Equal — all NaNs are one value here), and total_cmp(-0.0, 0.0) with total_cmp(1.0, 2.0) (both Less — the sign of zero is observed).
